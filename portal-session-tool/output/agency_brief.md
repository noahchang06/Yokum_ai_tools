# Agency Project Snapshot

## Public Problem
The Department of Natural and Cultural Resources manages a large network of public sites and programs across North Carolina, but it does not yet have a comprehensive estimate of their economic impact. DNCR wants to understand both statewide value and local community impact.

## Why It Matters
DNCR describes its sites as economic anchors, especially for rural communities. Better evidence could help the agency make the case for continued investment, support budget requests to the General Assembly, guide fundraising, and make strategic marketing or operations decisions.

## What Is Known
DNCR has more than 100 sites across the state, including historic sites, state parks, aquariums, museums, science museums, art museums, and the North Carolina Zoo. Some divisions, such as aquariums, the zoo, and state parks, have done individual assessments, but DNCR does not have a comprehensive department-wide analysis.

Available or potentially available data includes monthly visitation counts, operational budgets, site latitude and longitude, grant awards by program and county, VisitNC county tourism data, Hub spend data, and possibly procurement data with vendor zip codes. DNCR is also planning a visitor survey from Memorial Day to Labor Day.

## What Is Still Undefined
The final deadline is unclear from transcript. The preferred economic impact methodology is not specified. The transcript does not fully confirm whether procurement data can be provided with purchase amount, vendor, and vendor zip code. The exact scope of individual site reports is also still open.

Programs and grants are mentioned as valuable, but DNCR says site-level analysis is the must-have and program analysis is more of a nice-to-have.

## Possible Deliverables
- Statewide top-line estimate of DNCR economic impact
- Individual site-level reports or fact sheets
- Estimates of economic output, jobs supported, visitor spending, and local and state tax revenue
- Survey design recommendations before summer deployment
- Data quality review across visitation, procurement, grant, and location data
- Maps or dashboards showing site impact by geography

## Decisions This Could Support
The work could support budget requests, investment decisions, fundraising strategy, marketing by region, survey design, and decisions about whether to expand analysis from physical sites to programs and grants.
